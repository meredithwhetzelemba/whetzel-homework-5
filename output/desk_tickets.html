Reflection

How the agents performed

I do not grade a team for sounding busy. I grade it for the row it read, the chair it left empty, and whether the cash tab can survive the story. On that standard, ticket 102 was the only run I would hand to a landlord without a wince. Tickets 101 and 103 got the merchandise right and then spent extra model calls rereading it.

Ticket 101, run team_e21565a54f. Inventory earned her seat. Size S of CC-TEE-WHITE is 0 on hand against a request for 1, invoice 501 is the open $840.00 reprint due 2026-08-28, and shop today is 2026-08-31, so the bill was already late. Bulldog Print Co’s lead time of 5 days does not start while that invoice is open. Customer Service saved the holding note on the ticket and kept the $840.00 out of Tauhid’s draft. That is the part I trust. Accounting is the part I will not romanticize. Her order tool does not return checking, and when she asked Boss back, the depth cap stopped the chain. She said coverage was unknown. She did not invent the $3,400.00 that was sitting in cash_accounts at the reset. Honesty about a missing number is better than a made-up one. It is still an incomplete money answer on a ticket whose whole point was an unpaid bill. The human approve is what moved cash, from $3,400.00 to $2,560.00. The agents prepared that. They did not decide it.

Ticket 102, run team_ad0bf5677a. This is the run. Facilities named the Chapel Street shop, Elm City Properties, $2,400.00, and next due 2026-09-02, which is after shop today, so the notice is early, not overdue. Accounting read the rent tool after ticket 101 had already taken its $840.00, saw checking at $2,560.00, and said paying rent would leave $160.00. Look at the cash tab. That is the ending balance. The tool’s arithmetic and the payment row are the same number. Inventory and Customer Service stayed out, which is correct: a lease is not a SKU, and “Email:” in the note is how the notice arrived. Nobody wrote the landlord. I would keep this shape.

Ticket 103, run team_0e9ceebd8e. The facts are clean and the judgment is narrower than the flag they waved. Eight CC-HOOD-NAVY size M are on the shelf, twenty were asked for, twelve are short. List $58.00, cost $22.00, margin $36.00. No percent exists in the database, and nobody minted one. No vendor is on this ticket, and nobody borrowed Bulldog Print Co or Elm City Gifts to pretend the missing twelve had a house. The draft says eight are here and a bulk price is not quoted. Then Boss said no payment is due and the turn still came back needs_human_approval. The night desk has one signature line, and that line calls pay_approved_obligation. I signed it. The tool refused: a price override has no payable row. Checking stayed $160.00. The refusal is the tool being right. The button was the wrong instrument for a discount that was never a check.

Actual against the plan

On 101 the plan said Boss calls Inventory first, Inventory calls Accounting only after the zero and the open invoice, then Boss calls Customer Service, and Facilities stays home. That is what happened, plus a second Boss-to-Accounting call and Accounting’s bounce back to Boss. The tools match the plan: list_open_tickets, lookup_order_stock_and_reprint_invoice, save_board_draft, and not the pay tool. The plan also said Accounting should not pretend the order tool reveals checking. Actual obeyed that limit and then showed its cost: the cash tab’s first drop, $840.00, was a human decision made without the team ever stating that $3,400.00 could cover it. The plan was right about the shelf and the ship block. It was quiet about the depth cap eating the follow-up.

On 102 the plan and the run are the closest pair on this page. Boss called Facilities first. Facilities called Accounting. Inventory and Customer Service did not appear. Both specialists used lookup_rent_due_against_cash. The plan’s caution was that coverage of rent is coverage of rent alone, and that the $840.00 reprint plus rent would leave checking thin. Because we ran 101 first, Actual did not have to imagine that caution. The rent tool saw $2,560.00, not the opening $3,400.00, and named $160.00 left over. Boss also called the rent tool herself and called Accounting a second time. That is extra, not a different plan. The expected decision held: not overdue, do not pay until a human is named. The human was named. The cash tab records it.

On 103 the facts match the plan and the chain does not. The plan wanted Inventory first, then Inventory handing Accounting the 8-versus-20 before anyone talked price, then Boss handing Customer Service a draft with no percent. Actual has Boss calling Inventory, Accounting, and Customer Service herself. Four people, one tool, lookup_bulk_discount_margin, called over and over. The output is still the plan’s output: shortfall 12, margin $36.00, draft on the board, Facilities absent, no order tool, no rent tool, no pay. I will take a right answer in a sloppier shape. I will not pretend the shape was the one I wrote.

Where one agent would have been simpler

Ticket 102 is why the team exists. The lease question and the cash question are different sentences. Facilities should not be the person who touches checking, and Accounting should not be the person who decides what “Email:” means on a landlord’s notice. One agent with every tool would have been free to wander into the tee invoice while answering rent. The empty Inventory chair is the evidence the split worked.

Tickets 101 and 103 are where the split billed me for the same page five times. On 101, Boss, Inventory, Accounting, and Customer Service each called the order tool. The row did not change between calls: still 0 in size S, still invoice 501 at $840.00. The only new failure was structural. Agents do not hold get_checking_balance. That route belongs to the desk. So the specialist whose job was the bill could not see the account, and the delegation depth of 2 stopped her from asking someone who also could not see it. One agent, given the order tool, the draft tool, and a read-only checking lookup that is forbidden from appearing in a customer draft, would have said: size S is out, the vendor is blocked, $840.00 fits in $3,400.00, do not tell Tauhid the reprint cost, and wait for a signature. That is one pass. We took a committee.

On 103 the repetition is louder. The margin tool already returns on-hand, shortfall, cost, list, and margin. Inventory did not need to delegate to Accounting for a number that was in her own result, and Accounting did not learn a new fact by calling it again. A single agent with that tool and save_board_draft would have written the same draft: eight here, twelve short, no percent, no email. The team’s value on 103 was the empty Facilities chair and the refusal to invent a vendor. A well-prompted single agent can follow those two rules. I would keep the five roles for a day that mixes a lease and a shelf. I would not keep them for a day that is one hoodie row.

Invoice 501 is now paid, so Bulldog Print Co’s open-invoice count is 0. Inventory would report a shortfall of 0 and no ship block. Customer Service would draft a note that does not invent a delivery day. No one would call the rent tool. Second, the next rent demand against what is left. Lease 1 is still $2,400.00 and next_due is still 2026-09-02, because paying rent did not advance the date. Checking is $160.00. lookup_rent_due_against_cash already returns the balance, whether it covers the stored rent, and the leftover as a calculation. It would say coverage is false. pay_approved_obligation already refuses a payment that would drive checking negative, which is the same refusal we saw when ticket 103 had nothing to pay. Facilities and Accounting, and nobody else, can tell me not to sign September’s rent out of $160.00. That is the 102 play with the cash tab’s ending number instead of the opening one.

Third, the Yale club coming back for a quantity the shelf can fill. Size M has 8. Size L has 14. A new price_override for 8 size M, or for a count inside the 14 larges, is lookup_bulk_discount_margin again. Shortfall would be 0. Cost, list, and the $36.00 margin would still be the only price facts, because the database still has no percent. Customer Service can draft “we can fill this count at list” and stop. The pay tool should refuse again, as it did on 103, if someone confuses a price conversation with a check. I would want the approval flag to stay off unless a person is being asked for a price, and even then I would not want that click to touch the $160.00.

Three problems this team cannot take yet

First, the reprint arriving. The cash tab shows invoice 501 paid, $840.00 gone, status paid. The shelf does not care. CC-TEE-WHITE size S is still 0. Nothing we built receives goods. Paying a vendor is not the same sentence as putting a shirt in aisle B. I would add receive_paid_reprint, owned by Inventory, allowed only when the linked invoice is already paid and the ticket is a customer_order. It would add the ticket’s quantity to that SKU and size and refuse a second receipt. It would not touch checking. Until that tool exists, Tauhid’s draft is the true status: we cannot promise a day, and paying Bulldog did not put a small on the shelf.

Second, the drafts leaving the building. Ticket 101’s note to Tauhid and ticket 103’s note to the club are on the board with sent: false. That was the rule, and Customer Service kept it. There is no mailbox tool, and there should not be an automatic one. The problem we cannot solve is the ordinary one: the customer never hears the holding note. I would add release_board_draft, still owned by Customer Service, and it would flip sent only after a second human click that is not the pay button. It would refuse a draft that contains the reprint amount, a percent, or any figure Accounting did not store. Ticket 102 stays out of it. We do not email Elm City Properties from a line that begins “Email:”.

Third, a price that is a decision and not a withdrawal. Ticket 103 is the proof. The club asked for a bulk discount. The team correctly refused to invent one. The desk then offered me the same “Sign the check” control that paid $840.00 and $2,400.00, because needs_human_approval has one meaning in the model and the approve route always calls the pay tool. The tool saved the $160.00 by refusing. The shop still has no place to write “approved unit price, above the $22.00 cost, for the 8 we can fill.” I would add approve_shelf_price, owned by Accounting, taking a ticket id, a unit price, and a human name. It would refuse a price at or under cost, refuse a percent, refuse any ticket that is not a price_override, and it would not insert a payments row. The night desk would grow a second signature, so a hoodie conversation can never again look like rent.
