# The night desk

Campus Customs is a shop someone stays late to close. The dashboard is that desk, not a generic admin template and not a glitter “girl boss” poster. The look is an after-hours editorial office: near-black blotter, cream ticket paper, and one lipstick pink used only where a person has to act.

## What I looked at

I looked at current editorial dashboards rather than pastel SaaS kits.

- [Mind Studio’s editorial dashboard](https://mindstudioapps.com/en/lab/dashboard/dashboard-editorial) typesets an admin like a newspaper: warm paper, one serif for the numbers that matter, hairlines instead of cards, and the day’s figure as the headline. That is why checking leads the page and the tickets read as a short stack of notes.
- [My Brentano](https://styles.refero.design/style/d211aaa4-e09b-4ef9-a9bf-f6fa8495de73) keeps a cream sheet and near-black ink, and builds hierarchy with type size instead of shadows and rounded cards. The ticket numbers use that idea: the id is the big type, the subject is the small type.
- [Vuenix](https://demo.mycreativetemplates.com/vuenix/landing.html) treats Fraunces serif numerals the way a financial paper treats a figure. Checking is set in Fraunces italic so the money is the thing you notice when it changes.
- [Dispatch](https://www.rocket.new/templates/dispatch-bold-venture-capital-newsletter-landing-page-template) keeps burgundy rare on a black page so the accent stays a decision, not a theme. I used lipstick pink (`#e2186a`) the same way, because this course’s shop is black and pink. Pink is the run button, the Handled stamp, the selected ticket, and the balance after it drops. It is not the background.

The wordmark is “The night desk,” set italic, under a small-caps “Campus Customs.” Shop today sits under the title so the date on the blotter is the shop’s date, `2026-08-31`, not the computer’s clock.

## Layout

The page is one blotter, max about 1100px, with generous space and no sidebar.

1. Masthead. Title on the left. Checking on the right, large enough to read from across a table.
2. Three tickets. Ticket 101 is wider because it is the customer order. 102 and 103 sit beside it. Clicking one selects it with a pink outline.
3. The selected ticket’s note and two actions: “Send it to the team” and a quiet “Reset the book.”
4. Five staff plates in one row, Boss wider than the others, like the person at the head of the desk.
5. After the run, “What they decided” holds the Boss summary, the recommendation, and the signature line if money needs a human.

On a narrow screen the same pieces stack. Nothing important is hidden behind a menu.

## How the agents read differently

They share a row so you can watch all five at once, and they do not share a skin.

- **Boss** is the only full ink plate. She is the call. Her name is the largest on the row.
- **Inventory** sits on a warmer brown-black, like a shelf tag. The tool name under her line is the SKU-level work.
- **Accounting** uses Fraunces for what she says, so her plate reads as figures even when the sentence is short.
- **Facilities** is a dashed lease line, quieter than the others. Rent is a document, not a product.
- **Customer Service** is italic Fraunces on a deeper rose-black, the note slid across the desk. It should feel like stationery, not a chat bubble.

While a run is in progress, a plate with nothing yet says “At her desk.” When an audit event arrives for that role, the plate’s border turns pink and the line shows what she said plus the tool she used. A role that never speaks on that ticket ends as “She sat this one out.” That is deliberate: the plan does not send every specialist to every ticket, and the desk should show the empty chairs.

## Resolved tickets and cash

A finished run that is not refused marks the ticket resolved in the working database. The card warms from bright paper to a softer stock color and gets a tilted pink **Handled** stamp, the way a buyer stamps a folder, not a gray software badge. Open tickets just say Open.

Checking stays in the masthead the whole time. After an approved pay, the figure updates from `GET /cash` and turns pink for that drop, so the change is the color as well as the number. The signature line (“Sign the check”) only appears when the run asks for a human. The name you type is what the pay route records. Agents still do not move the cash.

Reset the book copies the original database back, which clears Handled stamps and puts checking back. It is on the desk because a person who just watched a payment wants to run the night again.
